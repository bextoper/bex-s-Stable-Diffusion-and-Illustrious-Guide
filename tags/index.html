Notable Tags

There are quite a few tags and combinations I and other folks use to achieve specific styles, actions, poses and such. Before listing some of them, note that tag behavior can differ from model to model. You and I might have different results using the same prompts, and it's fine. Let's get to it.

  • sketch, colorized - Style tags combination. This combination of tags will give your gens more of a drawn style, combatting a lot of that "default look" a lot of models have. !()[]
  • oekaki - A style tag. Similar to the above, but it's a more rough style, which might also bring a more pixelated look to your gens. It still often removes the "default look" of models, so it might be interesting for your purposes. !()[]
  • dancing - Action tag. It's not about dancing per se, but it's there to give a more dynamic and interesting pose to your characters.
  • muted color - A style tag. Use it to make the gens less colorful and less generic.
  • city, wall or street, wall - Background tag combination. Use it to get a proper shot of the character in a city/urban environment. Without wall, the character is often right in the middle of the road.
  • traditional media - An umbrella style tag. Use it to make your gens more drawn-like and less genetic. While it can work by itself, you might also be interested in more specific tags under it: Link.
  • good quality, best quality, masterpiece in the negatives. This one is very drastic and experimental, but if you get really sick of the default style, this one will for sure defeat it. I can't recommend that you stick to it, but it can give interesting results.
  • pixel-art - A style tag. Makes an effort for your gens to look like it's a pixel art, to various success.
  • greyscale and monochrome - Style tags. Both are used to make your gen monochrome, but besides that, it also introduced a lot of manga data into it, which might result in your gens getting a more rough, drawn look. Both are about the same, but greyscale is easier on various shades of grey, for shadows, for example.
  • spot color - A style tag, to be used together with greyscale or monochrome. Allows for one or two colors to appear in the monochrome gen. You can specify, for example, red hair, and pick what you want to color.
  • (character_name:0.2) - Character tag at low weight. You can try using it to make your own OC character, but with very small influence from the reference character. Might result in a more interesting looking characters.
  • no humans, scenery, either cityscape and/or nature, - Composition tags. Use them to make proper scenery gens without
  • negative space???? - test pending
  • couple - in multiple character gens, you often get images with the focus on just one character. This tag often makes it so that it's both characters depicted in a wider scene.
  • multiple views, collage, variations by @Cha
  • horror (theme) - A genre tag. The gens will lean more towards bringing horror elements into the roaster. I highly recommend weighing it down slightly, to 0.6 or 0.7.
  • limited palette - A style tag. Similar in effect to monochrome, but still allows some colors in the image. You can either rely on it completely, or specify some colors yourself.
  • no lineart, flat color - Style tags. The first one completely removes lines, and the second removes shadows/lighting differences/flattens colors in general. One more interesting style combination.
  • night/dark, shadow - Composition tags. shadow often produces really impressive and sometimes even coherent results.
  • rain/water, reflection - Composition tags. reflection often works incredible and is coherent.
  • bloom, blurry background, depth of field, photo background, - Composition tags, can be used separately. bloom adds more interesting lighting, blurry background does exactly what it says, depth of field is similar but more powerful, and photo background makes the background look more realistic, sometimes producing very good results.
  • black theme, red theme, etc - Composition tags. It determines the overall color theme of the image. Works especially well with various simple background tags, like abstract background.
